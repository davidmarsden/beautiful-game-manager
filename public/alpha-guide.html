01

What are TBG and TPF?

The Beautiful Game (TBG) is the shared game world: one continuing universe where managers run clubs, submit teams and tactics, negotiate transfers and live with the consequences.

The Pink Final (TPF) is the public player/profile/rating layer that feeds TBG. It is part of the same project philosophy: real-world evidence in, transparent and reproducible football judgements out.

The ambition is a deep, governed multiplayer football game where football judgement matters more than grinding, spending or hidden admin intervention. It may grow well beyond this alpha; it may also remain a smaller community experiment. There is no promise that it becomes a commercial Football Manager-scale product.

For the rules that apply to the game you can play now, read the Alpha Rulebook. For constitutional systems that are planned but not necessarily implemented yet, read The Road Ahead.

02

Your job as a tester

Play it like a manager and try to break it like a tester.

  • Use the portal normally and submit real decisions.
  • Try transfers, contracts, team selection and other market actions.
  • Tell us whenever something is confusing, surprising or broken.
  • Send screenshots separately when presentation or state looks wrong and one would help.
  • Use the in-game report form: it captures your manager, club, page, device and time automatically.

Real life comes first. Testing is not measured by how often you log in.

After a matchday

Five questions we actually care about

  1. What confused you?
  2. What was fun?
  3. What felt like work?
  4. What did you expect to be able to do but could not?
  5. What decision felt meaningless?
03

What to expect

This is an alpha, not a finished game. Rough edges, missing features and visible repairs are expected.

For the first cohort, some things are intentionally absent or incomplete, including cups, a richer economy, loans and three-club deals. More systems will be added only when the shared world proves it needs them.

The long-term goal is a persistent historical world, but during alpha we may have to repair state. In an extreme failure, a reset may still be necessary.

05

Privacy and transparency

TBG deliberately keeps its rules, governance and much of its source code public so managers can audit how the game works. Private game state is different: credentials, locked team selections, hidden scouting knowledge and other constitutionally private information stay protected.

For alpha operation we keep the minimum account information needed to authenticate and identify testers, including email/profile information, plus operational logs and in-game feedback reports needed to diagnose failures, security events and state changes. Those records are used to run and support the alpha, not to create a public manager directory.

If a problem involves private account information, send it directly to the inviter/admin rather than putting secrets into a report.

06

Known limits for the first cohort

  • No cups yet. The first controlled alpha is centred on the shared league world.
  • Economy is deliberately minimal. Canonical cash and wage affordability exist, but this is not yet a full financial simulation.
  • Loans and three-club deals are not yet part of the ordinary flow.
  • Presentation will keep changing. Some screens and explanations are still being refined as real managers use them.
  • State repair is possible. Alpha bugs may require an administrator to repair an appointment, transfer or other world state.
  • Screenshot upload is not in the first report form. Add the written report in-game first; screenshots can be shared separately when useful.
