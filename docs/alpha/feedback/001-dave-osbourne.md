**Source:** Retrospective email response supplied by the project owner

## Verbatim response

1. **What made you interested in trying TBG?** SM being so frustratingly unreliable (no proper iOS app), but otherwise fun to play.
2. **What did you most enjoy or find promising?** New kid on the block, am a big fan of cm01/02 (updated version), was looking forward to a simple manager game.
3. **What was confusing, frustrating, slow, or felt like work?** Too much text, complicated gameplay, wasn’t easy to navigate.
4. **How much did you care about your matches and results? What made you care more or less?** Not much, as long as it is fair and square.
5. **Was there a point when you stopped checking or playing regularly? What happened?** Too much text for me. See point 3.
6. **Compared with Soccer Manager Worlds / Top 100, what was TBG missing that mattered to you?** Graphics of some kind.
7. **Is there anything TBG did that you would genuinely miss if it disappeared?** Not yet.
8. **If we rebuilt it around a much simpler core — squad, transfers, team selection, tactics, match and league table — what would it absolutely need to make you want to keep playing?** Reliability and realistic gameplay (transfers, etc.), simple as cm01 really.
9. **Would you be willing to test a fresh version later?** Maybe, if I find time.
10. **Anything else you think we should learn from this alpha?** Nope, thanks for letting me test it.

## Coding and interpretation (analyst inference, not respondent quotations)

- **Primary friction — text/navigation:** Explicitly identifies too much text, complexity and difficult navigation; repeats this as reason for disengagement.
- **Expectation — simple management game:** Cites CM 01/02 as a positive reference, rather than demanding a feature-rich simulation.
- **Presentation — graphics:** Explicitly requests graphics of some kind; no particular graphics implementation specified.
- **Acquisition — SM reliability/iOS:** Existing Soccer Manager frustration motivated initial trial.
- **Match attachment — limited:** Says results mattered 'not much' so long as fair; do not infer the match engine was demonstrably unfair.
- **Retention/retest — weak:** No feature he would yet miss; only 'maybe' on returning, subject to time.
- **Reboot priorities — reliability, realistic transfers, simple interaction.**

## Reboot hypotheses to test (not established conclusions)

1. Prototype a football-first, visually legible manager interface: squad, formation, fixtures, results and league table should be immediately accessible; deeper explanations progressive rather than dominant.
2. Test whether a simpler interaction path reduces drop-off without sacrificing meaningful tactical/transfer depth.
3. Include perceived reliability and transfer realism in acceptance criteria; test iOS usability.
4. Ask other respondents about match emotional investment separately from perceived match fairness.

**Evidence discipline:** One retrospective response cannot establish prevalence or causation. Compare with product telemetry and later responses; do not imply this respondent endorsed a particular redesign.
