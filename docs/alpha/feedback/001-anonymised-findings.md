# Alpha 1 retrospective — anonymised findings 001

**Received:** October 2026  
**Evidence type:** Direct manager testimony, coded for the public project record  
**Privacy:** The source response and respondent identity are intentionally not stored in this repository.

## Findings relevant to the reboot

- **Text and navigation friction:** One respondent reported that the Alpha felt too text-heavy, complicated and difficult to navigate, and identified this as a reason for disengaging.
- **Expectation of simplicity:** The respondent had hoped for a comparatively simple football-management experience with familiar, straightforward interactions.
- **Presentation:** The respondent wanted some form of graphical presentation.
- **Acquisition opportunity:** Frustration with an existing game's reliability helped motivate interest in an alternative.
- **Match attachment:** The respondent reported limited emotional investment in match results while stressing that outcomes should feel fair.
- **Retention:** The respondent did not yet identify a TBG feature they would miss and was only conditionally open to testing a reboot.
- **Reboot needs:** Reliability, believable/realistic gameplay (including transfers), simplicity and usability emerged as stated priorities.

## Reboot hypotheses to test

These are analyst hypotheses derived from the testimony, not statements made or endorsed by the respondent.

1. Prototype a football-first, visually legible manager interface: squad, formation, fixtures, results and league table should be immediately accessible; deeper explanation should be progressive rather than dominant.
2. Test whether a simpler interaction path reduces drop-off without sacrificing meaningful tactical or transfer depth.
3. Include perceived reliability, transfer plausibility and mobile usability in acceptance criteria.
4. Measure match emotional investment separately from perceived match fairness.

## Evidence discipline

This is one retrospective response. It cannot establish prevalence or causation. Compare these themes with subsequent manager responses and behavioural evidence before treating them as Alpha-wide findings.

The original response is private correspondence and is deliberately excluded from the Git repository. Public reporting should remain anonymised unless the respondent explicitly consents to attribution.
