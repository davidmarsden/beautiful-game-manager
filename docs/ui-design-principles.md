# TBG UI design principles

## North star

**2026 interaction design, 1982 football soul.**

The Beautiful Game should not behave like a retro website. It should behave like a contemporary football-management product whose visual language comes from football before the Premier League era.

A second principle now sits alongside that: **classic game-screen composition, not full-width dashboard chrome.** On larger screens, the game should feel like a contained football world with breathing room around it. On phones, it should use the available width efficiently.

## Keep modern

- Navigation, hierarchy and responsive behaviour.
- Forms, buttons, focus states and accessibility.
- Loading, error and empty states.
- Touch targets and mobile discoverability.
- Clear feedback after actions.

## Palette and atmosphere

The Brazil palette remains part of TBG's identity: layered football greens, deep blue/navy working chrome, a bright yellow accent and clean light work surfaces. Green should provide atmosphere around and between the working areas rather than wash every individual component.

The aim is not to mimic Soccer Manager Worlds, but its older game-screen composition is a useful reference: a clear central canvas, dense information, a strong club/header strip and one dominant workspace per screen.

## Put the period character in the art direction

- Condensed programme-style display typography for headings and labels, paired with a modern UI sans for functional copy.
- Monospaced score/date/stat treatments where they help the football feel.
- Square, ruled layouts inspired by programmes, league tables and sports pages instead of generic rounded SaaS cards.
- Dense, readable tables that feel editorial rather than spreadsheet-like.
- Compact summary strips instead of making every piece of information a large dashboard card.
- One obvious primary workspace on each screen: squad table, team-selection pitch, inbox, transfer market, competition table, and so on.

## Responsive composition

- **Phone:** nearly full width, compact margins, touch-first controls and horizontal navigation where needed.
- **Tablet:** centred canvas at roughly 94% of the viewport so the interface has breathing room and does not feel like a wall of UI.
- **Desktop:** centred game canvas with a sensible maximum width and visible surrounding atmosphere.
- Do not make useful game data artificially narrow merely to imitate an older site.

## Avoid costume retro

Do not rely on sepia, fake paper damage, CRT scan lines, pixel fonts, novelty typewriter treatments or deliberately awkward controls. Retro character should come from composition, typography, colour and football references, not from making the product harder to use.

## Feature references

- **News:** sports pages / the football pink.
- **Squad:** programme team sheet / annual / sticker-album discipline.
- **Fixtures & results:** classified football scores and results columns.
- **Competitions:** dense newspaper league tables.
- **Matchday:** scoreboard / teleprinter restraint.
- **Manager profile:** annual or sticker-album detail, without sacrificing modern profile navigation.

## Test question

When reviewing a visual change, ask:

> Does this feel like a modern football game made by people who love football before the Premier League era, rather than a website built in 1998?

And on a tablet or desktop:

> Does the game have a clear canvas and hierarchy, or is every section shouting at me at full width?
