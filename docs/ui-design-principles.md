# TBG UI design principles

## North star

**2026 interaction design, 1982 football soul.**

The Beautiful Game should not behave like a retro website. It should behave like a contemporary football-management product whose visual language comes from football before the Premier League era.

## Keep modern

- Navigation, hierarchy and responsive behaviour.
- Forms, buttons, focus states and accessibility.
- Loading, error and empty states.
- Touch targets and mobile discoverability.
- Clear feedback after actions.

## Put the period character in the art direction

- Warm paper/newsprint surfaces rather than icy app chrome.
- Ink/navy/charcoal as the working colours, with a restrained mustard print accent.
- Condensed programme-style display typography for headings and labels, paired with a modern UI sans for functional copy.
- Monospaced score/date/stat treatments where they help the football feel.
- Square, ruled layouts inspired by programmes, league tables and sports pages instead of generic rounded SaaS cards.
- Dense, readable tables that feel editorial rather than spreadsheet-like.

## Avoid costume retro

Do not rely on sepia, fake paper damage, CRT scan lines, pixel fonts, novelty typewriter treatments or deliberately awkward controls. Retro character should come from composition, typography, colour and football references, not from making the product harder to use.

## Feature references

- **News:** sports pages / the football pink.
- **Squad:** programme team sheet / annual / sticker-album discipline.
- **Fixtures & results:** classified football scores and results columns.
- **Competitions:** dense newspaper league tables.
- **Matchday:** scoreboard / teleprinter restraint.
- **Manager profile:** annual or sticker-album detail, without sacrificing modern profile navigation.

## Test question

When reviewing a visual change, ask:

> Does this feel like a modern football game made by people who love football before the Premier League era, rather than a website built in 1998?
